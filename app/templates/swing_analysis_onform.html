Onform Body Data

How Onform Body Data Works · Coaching Interpretation

Use these measurements as context, not grades. Onform reconstructs 3D body motion from video. Turn describes rotation, sway describes target-line translation, lift describes vertical movement, and X-Factor is the rotational difference between torso and pelvis. The meaning changes by P-position, club and player.

Pelvis Turn

Lower-body rotation. In transition it helps show whether the pelvis leads the upper body; at impact it shows how open the lower body is. More is not automatically better—opening too aggressively can pull the handle and path left.

Torso Turn

Upper-torso rotation. Compare it with pelvis turn to understand separation and whether the chest is overtaking the lower body too early.

X-Factor

Torso turn minus pelvis turn. It describes rotational separation. For coaching, the change in separation during transition is often more useful than chasing one large number.

Torso / Pelvis Sway

Lateral movement toward or away from the target. It helps distinguish useful pressure/mass movement from a slide that can interfere with rotation, low point or face/path control.

Pelvis Lift

Vertical pelvis displacement from address. It helps identify lowering and extension patterns through the swing.

Tilt / Bend

Shoulder, pelvis and spine orientation provide context for side bend and delivery. These values should be read with turn and sway rather than in isolation.

Onform reference ranges: Green = within the typical professional range (mean ±1 SD); Orange = within ±2 SD but outside green; Red = outside ±2 SD. Onform separates references by male/female and driver/iron at Address, Top and Impact. These are comparison ranges—not mandatory positions or universal “good/bad” numbers.

Phase 5.2.7 · Sequencing

Onform Kinematic Sequence Review

The normal coaching reference is a proximal-to-distal peak order: pelvis → torso → lead arm → club. Deceleration of one segment while the next accelerates is part of effective energy transfer; do not coach every segment to keep accelerating to impact.
