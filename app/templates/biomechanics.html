COACHING PROCESS

Interpretation and decision sequence

Video → evidence → intervention → proof
1

Observe the movement

Begin with the coach-verified frames—not a presumed swing fault.

  • Confirm camera view, landmark placement and analysis confidence.
  • Identify the largest measured deviation and the phase where it occurs.
  • Describe what is visible before assigning a cause.

Decision: Is the pattern clear and repeatable enough to investigate?

2

Corroborate with ball flight

Use strike and Garmin outcome data to test the video hypothesis.

  • Face and start line establish the initial directional evidence.
  • Face-to-path, spin axis and offline finish describe curvature and outcome.
  • Check contact quality and multiple shots before blaming one body position.

Decision: Does the measured outcome support the movement finding?

3

Prescribe one priority

Choose the smallest useful intervention—not a complete swing rebuild.

  • Select one movement priority tied to the intended ball-flight change.
  • Use one clear cue and one constraint-led drill.
  • Define a measurable success target before the player starts.

Decision: What single change offers the highest value with the least disruption?

4

Retest under matching conditions

Repeat the same club, target and camera view for a fair comparison.

  • Record a five-shot group after rehearsal—not only the best swing.
  • Compare median start, curve, carry, strike efficiency and dispersion.
  • Keep the change only if outcome improves without a meaningful speed or contact penalty.

Decision: Keep, refine or discard the intervention based on measured results.
